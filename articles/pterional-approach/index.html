07 · Step 2 — danger zone

The facial nerve
sets the limit.

figure 3 · STA and TBFN↗ enlarge

Resident question

Why should the incision stay within 1 cm anterior to the tragus?

  • The TBFN runs above the anterior two-thirds of the zygomatic arch.
  • The most posterior branch of the facial nerve lies ~1.5 cm anterior to the tragus.
  • The STA is controlled early — and serves as a landmark for the TBFN.
Concept: the TBFN is found within 1 cm of the frontal branch of the STA above the zygomatic arch.08 / 19

Interfascial vs. subfascial

TechniqueKey points
Interfascial (Yaşargil)Superficial temporal fascia incised along the anterior STL; interfascial fat pad found ~4 cm above the lateral orbital rim; flap kept continuous with frontal pericranium.
SubfascialBoth fascia layers dissected from below the STL to the posterior zygomatic root; musculo-fascial cuff left along the STL; noted as safer for the TBFN.
Concept: loose areolar dissection stops at the superior edge of the suprafascial fat pad — beyond it, the plane is lost in the inferior temporal septum.10 / 19

10 · Step 4 — muscle

Take the muscle
down retrograde.

figure 5 · Oikawa technique↗ enlarge

Resident question

Why dissect the temporalis from inferior to superior?

  • Two cuts: vertical along the STL, transverse 1–2 cm below it — leaving the musculo-fascial cuff.
  • Retrograde elevation follows the muscle's natural plane.
  • Protects the deep temporal nerves and artery; minimizes postoperative atrophy.
Concept: the Oikawa technique reflects the temporalis caudally-to-rostrally over the zygomatic arch with fish hooks for maximal pericranial exposure.11 / 19

14 · Step 6 — drilling

Drill to the meningo-orbital
band — and stop.

figure 10 · extradural anterior clinoidectomy↗ enlarge

Two caveats

  • The orbital roof is thin — fracture it and orbital fat prolapses, requiring periorbita reconstruction.
  • The MOB is the medial limit of sphenoid wing drilling; locate and coagulate the meningo-orbital artery before any clinoidectomy.
Concept: detaching the MOB from the periorbita opens the middle fossa to the anterior clinoid — while protecting the cavernous sinus, optic nerve, and ICA.15 / 19

15 · Step 7 — intradural

Open the dura,
name the cistern.

figure 11 · after dural opening↗ enlarge

Name what you see

  • Sylvian fissure with the Sylvian vein.
  • Optic nerve (II) and internal carotid artery.
  • Horizontal segments of the anterior (A1) and middle (M1) cerebral arteries.
Concept: a C-shaped dural incision, convexity posterior; dural suspension prevents blood draining into the field and postoperative epidural hematoma.16 / 19
