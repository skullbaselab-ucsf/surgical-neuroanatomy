Mastoid tip–asterionfollows the digastric groove → approximates the sigmoid sinus

04 · Bony anatomy — figure 3

The posterolateral
landmarks.

figure 3 · bony anatomy of the RS approach↗ enlarge

Name what frames the approach

  • Asterion, EOP, superior and inferior nuchal lines.
  • Digastric groove, mastoid process, posterior condylar canal.
  • Inside: arcuate eminence, internal acoustic canal, sigmoid and transverse sulci.
Concept: the craniotomy lives between the transverse and sigmoid sinuses — every external landmark exists to find those two borders safely.05 / 19

08 · Step 5 — craniotomy, model 6

Expose the sinus
edges.

interactive model 6 · retrosigmoid craniotomy
Concept: the craniotomy exposes the TSSJ, the inferior edge of the TS, the posterior edge of the SS, and the posterior fossa floor — stopping short of the foramen magnum; every exposed mastoid air cell is waxed.09 / 19

09 · Step 5 — craniotomy variants, figure 4

Standard or
extended?

figure 4 · standard vs extended corridors↗ enlarge

What does skeletonizing the sinuses buy?

  • Standard RS: bone removed to the edges of the TS and SS.
  • Extended RS: both sinuses skeletonized, mobilized, and retracted with the dura.
  • Four burr holes: above the medial TS, above the TSSJ, near the jugular termination of the SS, and over the cerebellar hemisphere.
Concept: the extended RS widens both the working area and the angle of attack between cerebellum and petrous face — at the price of mastoidectomy and sinus handling.10 / 19

10 · Step 6 — dural opening, model 7

C, Y,
or X.

interactive model 7 · Y-shaped dural opening
Concept: a medially based C incision runs 3–5 mm off the TS and SS edges, keeping the sinuses covered for watertight closure; Y-shaped and X-shaped variants create dural leaves based on the sinuses.11 / 19

12 · Intradural anatomy — figure 6

Three triangles,
three corridors.

figure 6 · lateral triangles of the posterior fossa↗ enlarge

A corridor per cranial nerve group

  • Superior triangle — the CN V corridor: trigeminal neuralgia, SCA.
  • Central triangle — the CN VII–VIII complex: vestibular schwannoma, hemifacial spasm, AICA.
  • Inferior triangle — the lower cranial nerves IX–XII, PICA.
Concept: the article classifies RS surgical corridors by the cisternal segments of the cranial nerves — the approach is one, the corridor is chosen by the target nerve.13 / 19

13 · Keyhole RS — figure 7

Three keyholes,
three targets.

figure 7 · keyhole RS variations↗ enlarge

A 10–20 mm craniotomy, placed by target

  • Superior — triangular, at the inner curvature of the TSSJ: for CN V pathology.
  • Central — quadrangular, below the TS and medial to the SS: for CN VII–VIII.
  • Inferior — triangular, flush to the inferior SS and jugular bulb: for the lower cranial nerves.
Concept: the keyhole RS trades working area for precision — the same three intradural triangles, entered through a craniotomy a fraction of the size.14 / 19

15 · Intradural extensions — figure 8

Drill beyond
the meatus.

figure 8 · RISA and PIPA corridors↗ enlarge

How far can the RS reach?

  • RISA: remove the suprameatal tubercle → Meckel's cave, petroclival junction, posterior cavernous sinus.
  • PIPA: continue with a posterior intradural petrous apicectomy → anteromedial middle fossa, CN III, basilar trunk, anterolateral brainstem.
Concept: RISA suits pathology at the anterior border of the posterior fossa; PIPA is needed when the lesion spills into the posterior third of the cavernous sinus in the middle fossa.16 / 19

The suprameatal

figure 9 · RISA microsurgical sequence↗ enlarge
Follow along: identify the neurovascular frame around the SMT (AICA, SCA, SPV, CN V) → incise and dissect its dura → drill the tubercle → expose and open the posteroinferior porus trigeminus.17 / 19

17 · PIPA — figure 10

The petrous apex
from behind.

figure 10 · PIPA microsurgical view↗ enlarge

Limits of the apicectomy

  • Superior: tentorium and superior petrosal sinus.
  • Anteromedial: CN VI, Dorello's canal, posterior cavernous sinus.
  • Inferior: inferior petrosal sinus; posterolateral: porus trigeminus.
Concept: CN V is protected with a rubber dam while the apex comes out with a diamond burr — Meckel's cave opens once the tentorial free edge is resected parallel to the petrous ridge.18 / 19
