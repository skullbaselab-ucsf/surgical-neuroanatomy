05 · Step 3 — fascial dissection, figure 3

Stay in
the right plane.

figure 3 · fascial dissection↗ enlarge

Resident question

Interfascial or subfascial — where is the facial nerve safest?

  • Incision crosses the superficial temporal fascia below the STL and the frontal pericranium above it.
  • The plane stays between temporal fascia and temporalis — both fascial layers elevate with the fat pad.
  • Either way, the TBFN travels inside the elevated flap, protected.
Concept: the subfascial variant elevates both fasciae and the fat pad in a single step — simple, efficient, and the safest for the TBFN.06 / 19

06 · Steps 3–4 — fascial topography, figure 4

Detach the fascia
from bone.

figure 4 · intrafascial and subfascial overview↗ enlarge

Why the axial view matters

  • The superficial fascial layer typically continues with the periosteum.
  • The deep layer tends to attach to the inferior border of the bone.
  • So detachment from the zygoma and orbital rim must follow each layer's own insertion.
Concept: after the intra- or subfascial dissection, freeing the fascial layers from the zygoma and orbital rim is the critical step before the craniotomy.07 / 19

07 · Two-piece FTOZ — figure 5

First the flap,
then the orbit.

figure 5 · two-piece FTOZ approach↗ enlarge

The sequence, A to E

  • Deep temporal fascia dissection exposes the zygomatic arch and lateral orbital rim.
  • Temporalis elevated inferiorly; periorbita dissected off the orbital walls to the IOF.
  • First bone piece: the standard pterional flap.
  • Orbital, anterior zygomatic, and posterior zygomatic cuts release the second piece.
  • Frontal and temporal dura and periorbita exposed.
Concept: the MacCarty keyhole and the IOF — the two landmarks that organize the entire two-piece FTOZ.08 / 19

13 · Three-piece FTOZ — figure 7

Recline the arch,
free the orbit.

figure 7 · three-piece FTOZ approach↗ enlarge

Three pieces, in order

  • Piece 1: the zygomatic arch — cut posterior to the zygomaticotemporal suture and anterior to the TMJ, reclined inferiorly with the masseter.
  • Piece 2: the standard pterional flap.
  • Piece 3: orbital rim, orbital roof, and lateral orbital wall — the roof cut extends around the SOF toward the IOF.
Concept: Campero's three-piece buys exposure of the middle fossa floor, lateral orbital rim, and IOF at similar operative time and complication rates.14 / 19
