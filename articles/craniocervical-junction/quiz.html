Resident case quiz · discussion prompts

Craniocervical Junction

Educational discussion prompts based on Vigo et al., Immersive Surgical Anatomy of the Craniocervical Junction, Cureus 2020. These are synthetic teaching cases, not patient-specific recommendations.

Case 1The posterior C1 arch

During a posterior C1 exposure, the resident identifies a groove on the superior surface of the posterior arch.

  1. Which vessel occupies this groove?
  2. Which nerve is closely related?
  3. What anatomic variant could convert the groove into a canal?
Reveal answer

The groove contains the V3 segment of the vertebral artery; the C1 dorsal ramus travels anterior to the artery in this region. Ponticulus posticus can partially or completely enclose the groove, creating an arcuate foramen.

Case 2The dens and instability

A resident describes the transverse ligament as “just another ligament around C1–C2.”

  1. What is the key mechanical function of the transverse component of the cruciform ligament?
  2. What imaging relationships may change when it fails?
Reveal answer

It holds the dens against the posterior surface of the anterior arch of C1 and limits anterior displacement of the atlas. The article describes an increased atlantodental space above 3 mm or a reduced distance between the posterior dens and posterior C1 ring below 13 mm as relationships associated with transverse-ligament failure.

Case 3The unexpected odontoid

Imaging shows a separate odontoid fragment rather than a fused dens–C2 body complex.

  1. What developmental variant should be considered?
  2. Why does the finding matter for a textbook-based mental model?
Reveal answer

An os odontoideum should be considered, reflecting failed fusion at the subdental synchondrosis. It matters because the expected dens, C1 relationship, and stability assumptions may not apply.

Case 4Choosing the corridor

A lesion is being evaluated for an anterior approach. The team is debating an endoscopic endonasal versus transoral route.

  1. What is the role of the hard-palate line in the article’s framework?
  2. Which additional lines help define the endonasal and transoral limits?
Reveal answer

The article describes the endoscopic endonasal approach for lesions above the hard-palate line and the transoral approach for lesions below it. Nasopalatine, nasoaxial, and rhinopalatine lines help define the inferior endonasal limit; palatine inferior dental arch and atlantosuperior dental arch lines help define transoral limits. Patient-specific imaging remains necessary.

Case 5The motion question

A resident says that the CCJ is stable because it does not move much.

  1. Approximately how much rotation does the CCJ permit?
  2. Which articulation supplies most of the head’s rotation?
  3. Name two structures that help constrain excessive motion.
Reveal answer

The article reports approximately 90° of rotation overall. The atlantoaxial articulation supplies most of the rotation, with the atlas ring rotating around the dens. The alar ligaments, tectorial membrane, and transverse ligament are among the structures that constrain excessive movement.

Case 6The preoperative five

Before a difficult CCJ case, the attending asks for a five-part verbal map.

  1. What five domains should the resident name before discussing the approach?
Reveal answer

Bone, ligament, artery, nerve, and geometry: the osseous corridor; the stabilizing structures; the vertebral-artery course and variants; nearby neural structures; and the imaging lines / angles that determine access and alignment.

Facilitator use

  • Ask the question before revealing the model annotation.
  • Let the resident rotate the model before supplying the answer.
  • Require a spatial explanation (“where is it?”) in addition to a structure name (“what is it?”).
  • Use the answer key to guide discussion, not to assign a score.
